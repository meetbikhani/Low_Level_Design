Your Game class can contain the Board, the two Players, the currentPlayer, and the GameStatus enum (RUNNING, GAME_OVER). Its job should mainly be to orchestrate the game, so play() can run while the game is running, take the current player's move, ask the board to process it, check the resulting game condition, and then switch the current player. changePlayer() simply changes between the two players. I would avoid putting detailed chess logic inside Game; Game should coordinate rather than know how a bishop or knight moves.

Your Board class is the most important part. It can contain an 8×8 collection of Cells and expose something like makeMove(source, destination). The board should validate that the source/destination are inside the board, check that there is actually a piece at the source, check whether the destination contains a friendly piece, ask the piece whether the movement pattern is valid, and then perform the move. However, I'd separate "is this movement pattern valid?" from "is this move legal in the current chess position?". For example, a bishop can mathematically move diagonally, but the path might contain another piece. Similarly, a king cannot make a move that puts itself in check. So your Piece.isValid() should primarily answer whether the piece can move from (sourceRow, sourceCol) to (destRow, destCol) according to its movement rule, while Board/a dedicated MoveValidator can handle board-level rules such as path obstruction, friendly pieces, capturing, and whether the move leaves the king in check.

Your Cell design is good: it can simply contain row, column, and the currently occupying Piece, where the piece can be null. Your Piece interface can have something like isValidMove(source, destination) along with getColor()/getName(). Then King, Queen, Rook, Bishop, Knight, and Pawn implement their respective movement rules. One important point: don't make each piece responsible for checking the entire board. For example, the bishop shouldn't itself search through every cell to determine whether something is blocking its path. The bishop knows "I move diagonally"; the board/validator knows "is the diagonal path clear?"

Your Player class containing name and color is also correct. The player's color should determine which pieces they are allowed to move. You could optionally have the player's pieces, but I wouldn't necessarily store them because the board already represents the current game state. When a player says makeMove(e2, e4), the system can retrieve the piece from the board, verify that its color matches the current player, and then ask the board to execute the move.

For check and checkmate, I would improve your design slightly. Instead of having Board become responsible for every complicated chess rule, you could introduce a ChessRuleValidator or MoveValidator. It can have methods such as isValidMove(), isInCheck(), and isCheckmate(). This keeps the board focused on storing and updating pieces, while the validator focuses on chess rules. This becomes particularly useful because checkmate isn't just a property of the board—it requires considering the king, opponent attacks, and whether the player has any legal move remaining.

For the Observer Pattern, your idea is good. You can have the game publish events such as "move made", "check", "checkmate", or "game over", and observers can react. One observer could display the board, another could provide commentary, and another could maintain a move history or send notifications. I would actually avoid making the observer responsible for the core game logic; it should only react to events after something happens.
