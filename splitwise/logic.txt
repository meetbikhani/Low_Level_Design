I'm thinking of designing the Splitwise system with a central Splitwise class acting as the entry point for managing users, groups, expenses, balances, and settlements. Users can be created and added to groups, where each Group maintains its members and a history of expenses. Whenever someone pays for an expense, an Expense object is created containing details such as the total amount, description, payer, participants, and their respective splits. The calculation of how an expense is divided would use the Strategy pattern, allowing different implementations such as EqualSplit, PercentageSplit, and ExactAmountSplit. Each expense would generate Split objects representing how much every participant owes, and the system would maintain a balance sheet to track debts between users. A separate Transaction or Settlement class would represent the actual transfer of money from one user to another, which is different from an expense itself. Groups can maintain their expense history, while users can view their individual balances and transaction history. Optionally, the Observer pattern can be used to notify group members whenever a new expense is added or a settlement is completed. 




To simplify debts in Splitwise, we first calculate the net balance of every user and separate them into debtors (users with a negative balance who need to pay money) and creditors (users with a positive balance who need to receive money). For example, suppose A owes ₹500, B owes ₹300, C needs to receive ₹600, and D needs to receive ₹200. We put debtors and creditors into separate priority queues so that we always pick the person owing the maximum amount and the person receiving the maximum amount. First, A owes ₹500 and C needs ₹600, so we settle the minimum of both amounts, ₹500, meaning A pays C ₹500; A is now settled while C still needs ₹100. Next, B owes ₹300 and D needs ₹200, so B pays D ₹200, leaving B with ₹100 to pay. Finally, B pays the remaining ₹100 to C. Therefore, instead of maintaining many complex transactions between users, we only look at everyone's final net balance and repeatedly match a debtor with a creditor using min(debt, credit) until both queues become empty. This gives the simplified transactions: A → C ₹500, B → D ₹200, and B → C ₹100.
