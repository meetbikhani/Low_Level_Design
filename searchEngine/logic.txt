We are designing a simple in-memory Search Engine LLD that supports both exact keyword search and substring search over a predefined collection of documents or webpages. Since the documents are already available, we don't need to implement web crawling, which is the process of automatically discovering and downloading webpages. We'll have a Document class containing the document ID, title and content, and a Posting class containing the document ID and the frequency of a particular word in that document. The Indexer will process each document by converting its content to lowercase, splitting it into individual words and building two indexes: an Inverted Index and a Trigram Index. The Inverted Index will use a HashMap<String, List<Posting>>, mapping complete words to the documents containing them and their frequencies, allowing exact keyword searches such as programming. The Trigram Index will map groups of three consecutive characters to document IDs, allowing substring searches such as gram to find words like programming. For example, programming generates trigrams such as pro, rog, ogr, gra, ram, amm, mmi, min and ing. When a substring query is received, the SubstringSearchService will generate its trigrams, retrieve the corresponding posting lists, intersect the candidate document IDs and verify that the original substring actually exists in the document's indexed words before returning results. For queries shorter than three characters, we'll use a fallback approach such as scanning the indexed vocabulary. The SearchService will handle exact keyword searches, while the SubstringSearchService will handle substring searches. Both will use a common frequency-based ranking mechanism to rank matching documents by how frequently the searched word or substring occurs, returning the most relevant results first. The SearchEngine will act as the main entry point, initialize the predefined documents, coordinate the Indexer, and expose the search functionality. We'll keep matching case-insensitive, treat words equally without stop-word removal or stemming, and use predefined queries rather than runtime user input. We won't implement fuzzy search, which handles spelling mistakes, or phrase queries, which search for exact sequences of consecutive words, in this version. The focus will be on maintaining separate indexes for exact and substring matching while keeping indexing, searching and ranking responsibilities clearly separated.
